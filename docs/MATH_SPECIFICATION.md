## 2. Definitions & conventions

- **Stake (S):** the total amount wagered on a spin. The player selects one of
  the stake levels `{0.20, 0.50, 1.00, 2.00, 5.00, 10.00, 20.00, 50.00}`. All
  pay values and feature awards in this document are expressed as multiples of
  **S** (the *total* stake — there is no per‑line concept in a ways game).
- **Screen / grid:** the 5×3 array of symbols visible after the reels stop.
  `grid[r][row]`, `r ∈ {0..4}` (reel), `row ∈ {0..2}`.
- **Way:** a left‑to‑right chain of a single paying symbol on consecutive reels
  beginning at reel 1.
- **Span:** the number of consecutive reels (from reel 1) a symbol occupies
  (must be ≥ 3 to pay).
- **Cycle:** one full enumeration of the base‑game outcome space (see §10).

## 3. Symbols & paytable

There are 15 symbol identities: 3 Hard Hats (also the scatter), 6 themed
high/mid symbols, 5 card royals (low pay), and 1 Wild.

Pays are a **per‑way multiple of total stake S**. A symbol's contribution to a
spin is `ways × pay[span] × S` (see §5).

**Notes**

- The **Wild has no pay of its own**; it only substitutes (§4).
- The three **Hard Hats are dual‑purpose**: they pay as ordinary ways‑symbols
  *and* act as the bonus scatter (§6). Wilds do **not** substitute for hats.
- Royals carry the base‑game hit frequency; the high symbols and the wild carry
  the base‑game volatility.
- Line pays were deliberately scaled to **0.51×** an earlier revision so that the
  return added by the expanding wild does not push the total RTP above target
  (see §8.2). Any change to a pay value must be re‑verified with `tools/sim.js`.

## 4. Wild symbol & expansion rule

The **Wolf Wild** is an *expanding* wild placed on the **centre reel (reel 3)**
only. (The evaluation engine supports wilds on any of the centre reels 2–4; the
current par sheet places exactly one wild, on reel 3 — see §7.)

**Expansion rule (`expandWilds`, `mathcore.js`):**

1. If a Wild appears anywhere in a reel's 3‑cell window, the **entire reel**
   becomes Wild for evaluation purposes…
2. …**except** any Hard‑Hat cells on that reel, which are preserved unchanged
   (so the wild never removes or adds scatters — the bonus‑trigger probability
   is independent of wild expansion).
3. A Wild substitutes for **every paying symbol except the three Hard Hats**.
4. The Wild contributes **no pay of its own**.

Expansion is applied **idempotently inside `evaluateGrid`**, so evaluation is
identical whether the caller passes the raw or the pre‑expanded grid. The same
expansion applies in the base game **and** during free spins.

**Modelling consequence.** Because the Wild has no pay, its entire RTP
contribution is realised through the symbols it *extends*. Reel 3 carries the
most frequent symbols (royals), so when reel 3 expands, royal ways frequently
extend through reels 1‑2‑3(‑4‑5). This is why, in the per‑symbol contribution
table (§8.3), the **royals dominate** the base‑game RTP — that share is largely
the wild's effect, not ordinary royal line hits. P(a Wild is visible on reel 3)
= 3 × 1 / 33 = **9.09%** per spin.

## 5. Win evaluation (243 ways)

Evaluation is left‑to‑right, ways‑pay. For each paying symbol *X* (every symbol
with a paytable entry, i.e. all but the Wild):

```
g            = expandWilds(grid)              // wild reels become wild (hats kept)
matches(s)   = (s == X) OR (X is not a hat AND s == WILD)
colCount[r]  = number of cells in reel r where matches() is true
if colCount[0] == 0:  X does not pay (must start on reel 1)
span = 1; ways = colCount[0]
for r = 1..4:
    if colCount[r] == 0: break
    span += 1; ways *= colCount[r]
if span >= 3 and pay[X][span] exists:
    win_X = ways × pay[X][span] × S
totalWin = Σ_X win_X
```

**Rules that follow from the algorithm:**

- A symbol pays on its **single longest left‑anchored span** only (a symbol is
  not paid separately for a shorter sub‑span).
- **Ways** for a span = the product of the symbol's (incl. substituting wild)
  occurrence counts on each covered reel.
- Multiple *different* symbols can each pay on the same spin (their wins sum).
- Hats pay as ways **and** count toward the trigger.
- There is no scatter‑pay separate from the ways pay.

**Worked example (with wild expansion).** Suppose after expansion the Ace counts
per reel are `(2, 1, 3, 1, 0)` — i.e. reel 3 is a fully‑expanded wild reel
(3 substituting cells), Ace on reels 1, 2, 4, none on reel 5.
Span = 4 (reels 1‑4 non‑zero, reel 5 zero), ways = 2·1·3·1 = 6,
pay[Ace][4] = 0.89 → win = 6 × 0.89 × S = **5.34 × S**.

## 6. Bonus feature — *Hard Hat Free Spins*

The live presentation (`src/game/bonus.js`) and the headless model
(`simulateBonusOutcome` in `mathcore.js`) implement the **same money logic**;
the headless model is the certifiable reference.

### 6.1 Trigger

- **6 or more Hard Hats** (any of yellow/green/red, any positions) on a base
  spin trigger the bonus and award **6 free spins**.
- The hat count is taken on the *post‑expansion* grid, but since expansion
  preserves hats this equals the raw hat count.
- **On the triggering spin, base ways wins are forfeited** — the spin pays the
  bonus only. (The base game early‑returns into the feature; the verifier models
  this exactly — see §8.1.)
- Measured trigger rate: **≈ 1 in 179 spins**.

### 6.2 Free spins, houses & retrigger

Each trigger hat marks its cell with a **tier‑1 (straw) house frame**. Then, for
each free spin:

1. A normal 5×3 screen is drawn and **pays ordinary 243‑ways wins** (same
   paytable, wild expansion active); this is added to the bonus total.
2. **Each hat that lands upgrades the house on its cell by one tier**, capped at
   tier 3: straw (1) → stick (2) → brick (3). Frames are **persistent** for the
   whole feature.
3. **Retrigger:** if **3 or more hats** land in a single free spin, **+1 free
   spin** is added.
4. **Mansion Jackpot check** (see 6.4).

Measured average length: **≈ 7.3 free spins** per bonus.

### 6.3 Wolf reveal — house awards

When the free spins end, the wolf "huffs and puffs" and **every built house is
blown down for a cash award**, drawn independently per house from the tier
distribution below (`BONUS_CONFIG.tiers`, `rollHouseAward`):

| Tier | House | Base award | Jackpot branch | E[award] |
|--:|---|---|---|--:|
| 1 | 🏚️ Straw | `S × U(0.46, 2.44)` | — | **1.45 × S** |
| 2 | 🏠 Stick | `S × U(2.44, 9.74)` | 4% → `S × 29` (Mini) | **7.01 × S** |
| 3 | 🏰 Brick | `S × U(7.31, 44.08)` | 4% → `S × 146` (Minor) | **30.51 × S** |

`U(a,b)` = continuous uniform on `[a,b]`. Expectations:
`E₁ = (0.46+2.44)/2 = 1.45`;
`E₂ = 0.04·29 + 0.96·(2.44+9.74)/2 = 7.01`;
`E₃ = 0.04·146 + 0.96·(7.31+44.08)/2 = 30.51`.

### 6.4 Mansion Jackpot

When a **fresh brick** is created **and 3+ brick houses** are present on the
grid, a Mansion Jackpot fires **once** for that spin:

```
mansion award = S × ( 24.4 + U(0, 19.7 × brickCount) )       (BONUS_CONFIG.mansion)
E[mansion | fires] = S × ( 24.4 + 9.85 × brickCount )
```

Measured frequency: **≈ 0.233 mansion awards per bonus**.

### 6.5 Total bonus value

`bonus total = Σ(free‑spin ways wins) + Σ(mansion awards) + Σ(house awards)`.

Measured **E[bonus | trigger] ≈ 85.5 × S** (σ is large — the tier‑3 and mansion
jackpots produce the heavy tail). Bonus RTP contribution = trigger‑rate ×
E[bonus | trigger] = (1/179) × 85.5 ≈ **47.6%**.

## 7. Reel strips (par sheet)

Reels are independent. For each reel a single stop position is drawn uniformly
on `{0 … L−1}` (L = strip length) and the 3 visible cells are positions
`stop, stop+1, stop+2` (mod L). Strips are generated deterministically by
`buildStrip()` from the per‑reel symbol counts: fillers are interleaved
round‑robin and the hats are dropped in **2‑symbol clusters**, so a 3‑cell
window can show 2 hats at once (this is what makes a 6‑hat trigger reachable).

symbols it is the expected number of cells (which can be 0–3). Shown as a

## 8. RTP analysis

### 8.1 Accounting convention (must match the game)

A spin that triggers the bonus pays **bonus‑only**; its base ways wins are not
paid (§6.1). The verifier (`tools/sim.js`) and the in‑game estimator
(`src/panels/simulator.js`) both implement this: on a 6+‑hat spin they credit the
bonus and **do not** credit base ways wins. This makes the reported **base RTP an
accurate reflection of what players actually receive**, not a theoretical
base‑plus‑forfeited‑trigger figure.

### 8.2 Headline RTP

| Component | RTP |
|---|--:|
| **Total** | **96.99%** |
| Base game (ways + expanding wild) | 49.37% |
| Bonus feature | 47.62% |

50,000,000‑spin Monte‑Carlo estimate at S = 1. Standard error of the mean ≈
σ/√N = 9.26/√(5·10⁷) ≈ **0.13%** (1σ); i.e. 96.99% ± 0.13%. Independent runs
of 10–50M spins fall in **96.3% – 97.2%**, consistent with a true value of
**≈ 97%** and the feature's heavy tail. The design target is **97.0%**.

Within the **base game**, ordinary line hits contribute ≈ 30% and the expanding
wild contributes ≈ 19% (the wild's value appears inside the symbols it
substitutes — see §4 and §8.3).

### 8.3 Base‑game RTP contribution by symbol

Share of total stake returned by each symbol in the base game (20M spins). The
Wild reads 0% by construction (no own pay); its value is embedded in the symbols
it extends — chiefly the royals, which is why royals lead this table.

| Symbol | RTP contribution |
|---|--:|
| Ten | 15.15% |
| King | 7.94% |
| Ace | 7.54% |
| Jack | 6.67% |
| Queen | 6.67% |
| Toolbox | 1.34% |
| Wolf | 1.32% |
| Suit Pig | 1.19% |
| Builder Pig | 0.90% |
| Shotglass | 0.30% |
| Yellow Hat | 0.15% |
| Buzzard | 0.13% |
| Red Hat | 0.06% |
| Green Hat | 0.04% |
| Wild | 0.00% |
| **Base total** | **≈ 49.4%** |

### 8.4 Volatility, hit frequency & win distribution

| Metric | Value |
|---|--:|
| σ of per‑spin return (× S) | **9.26** |
| Hit frequency (any win > 0) | 26.43% |
| Mean return per spin | 0.9699 × S |
| Largest win in 50M spins | 1,274 × S |

The standard deviation of ~9.3× stake places the game firmly in the **high
volatility** band. The distribution is heavily right‑skewed: most paid spins are
small base wins, while the upper tail is driven by tier‑3 brick jackpots (146×),
Stick/Mini jackpots (29×) and the Mansion Jackpot.

## 9. RNG, fairness & exposure

### 9.1 Outcome generation model

Each reel draws **one independent, uniform** stop on its strip; the screen is the
five resulting 3‑cell windows. All randomness in the bonus (uniform award draws,
4% jackpot Bernoulli branches) is independent of reel outcomes. The math model
assumes a source of independent uniform variates.

### 9.2 RNG implementation — **action required for certification**

> ⚠️ The current reference implementation (both the live game and the verifier)
> uses the JavaScript `Math.random()` PRNG. **`Math.random()` is not a
> cryptographic or certified RNG and must not be used for real‑money play.** For
> certification/deployment, the reel‑stop and award draws must be sourced from a
> **GLI‑certified RNG** (uniform, well‑seeded, with documented scaling/mapping).
> The math model in this document is RNG‑agnostic: it depends only on the
> assumption of independent uniform draws, so substituting a certified RNG does
> not change any RTP/volatility figure herein.

### 9.3 Independence & fairness properties

- Reel stops are mutually independent and uniform → every one of the 36,729,792
  base screens is equally likely.
- Wild expansion and bonus awards are deterministic functions of the draws plus
  independent uniforms; there is no adaptive/compensated behaviour and no
  dependence on player history, balance, or stake (awards scale linearly with S).

### 9.4 Maximum exposure — **note for the reviewer**

There is **no explicit max‑win cap** in the code. The largest observed win in
50M spins was 1,274 × S, but the theoretical maximum is far higher (a maximally
upgraded board: up to 15 brick houses with jackpot branches, plus Mansion and
free‑spin line wins). For jurisdictions that require a published max‑win or a
cap, a cap should be specified and added; the worst‑case combinatorial exposure
should be computed before certification.

## 10. Verification methodology

### 10.1 Reproduce the figures (headless)

```
node tools/sim.js 50000000      # RTP (base/bonus/total), trigger rate, hit freq,
                                # volatility, per‑symbol contribution, reel
                                # composition, and bonus‑buy pricing
```

`tools/sim.js` imports the **same** `par-sheet.js` and `mathcore.js` the live
game is bundled from, so the numbers are the game's real numbers (not a
re‑implementation).

### 10.2 Recommended independent checks

1. **Exact base‑game RTP (full cycle).** The base outcome space is only
   36,729,792 equally‑likely screens. Enumerate every screen, evaluate with the
   §5 algorithm (treating 6+‑hat screens as bonus‑only per §8.1), and divide by
   `36,729,792 × S`. This yields the base‑game RTP to full precision and removes
   all Monte‑Carlo error on the base component.
2. **Analytic cross‑check of bonus expectation.** Using the per‑tier
   expectations in §6.3–6.4 and a Markov model of frame upgrades across free
   spins (states = per‑cell tier; transitions driven by per‑reel hat
   probabilities in §7.3, plus the retrigger rule), compute `E[bonus | trigger]`
   and compare to the ≈ 85.5 × S Monte‑Carlo figure.
3. **Re‑derive the trigger rate** from the hat distribution / clustering in §7
   and compare to the ≈ 1/179 measurement.
4. **Independent Monte‑Carlo** in a separate language using the strips in §7.2
   and the paytable in §3 — should reproduce 96.99% ± 0.13%.

### 10.3 In‑game tools (same math)

- **🧮 MATH** panel — plain‑English RTP / trigger / composition (runs a 2M‑spin
  estimate live).
- **📊 SIM** panel — full Monte‑Carlo dashboard with charts.

Both call the same `mathcore.js`, so all three agree within Monte‑Carlo noise.

## 11. Bonus Buy

The player may pay **88 × S** to enter the *Hard Hat Free Spins* feature
immediately (UI: **💰 BUY BONUS**).

```
fair price = E[bonus | trigger] / game RTP = 85.47 / 0.9699 ≈ 88.1 × S
buy RTP    = E[bonus | trigger] / price    = 85.47 / 88     ≈ 97.1%
```

The purchased bonus is started from a trigger screen drawn from the **same
distribution** as a naturally‑occurring trigger, so the bought bonus is
statistically identical to a spun one. The buy RTP (≈ 97.1%) is set to match the
base‑game RTP (≈ 97.0%) so buying is value‑neutral.

> Jurisdictional note: some regulators restrict or prohibit "feature buy"
> mechanics. The buy can be disabled via configuration without affecting any
> other math.
